The system

What orthokeratology is, and where VIPOK fits

Orthokeratology reduces refractive error and improves unaided vision by using rigid gas permeable (RGP) lenses to gradually reshape the cornea. The effect is temporary: the cornea returns toward its original shape when lens wear stops.

How ortho-k designs have evolved

Since the 1960s, ortho-k lens designs have moved from traditional fits flatter than the cornea to multi-curve reverse geometry designs. VIPOK lenses use a four-curve, double reverse geometry design. Each generation needs more understanding to fit and troubleshoot.

Lens geometry

VIPOK™ and VIPOK™ II are four-curve, double reverse geometry lenses: an optic zone (OZ), reverse curve (RC), alignment curve (AC) and peripheral curve (PC).

Reading the fit

Ideal VIPOK fit = bullseye fluorescein pattern + good centration + 0.1 mm movement with the blink.

Ortho-k and laser vision correction

Both change the shape of the cornea to change refraction, in different ways. Laser vision correction is surgery and its effect is permanent. Ortho-k’s effect is temporary and reverses when lens wear stops.

Which option suits a patient is a clinical decision for the eye care practitioner, within each device’s approved indications.

Success depends on training: practitioners need proper instruction before fitting, and patients need to learn lens care and keep up with follow-up visits. Overnight lens wear carries risks, including serious eye infection; see Important Safety Information.

For practitioners
Frequently asked questions

    

      
What is different about the VIPOK™ design and system?
Two things. First, a reference chart and inventory system for fitting and dispensing from stock. Second, the practitioner selects and adjusts the lens chairside, instead of waiting on a lab to recalculate.

      
Where is the VIPOK™ design available?
Availability depends on regulatory approval in each country. Contact VIPOK to confirm availability where you practice.

      
How much office space does VIPOK™ ortho-k need?
The same as normal contact lens fitting and dispensing: an examination room, a teaching room, and a waiting and file storage area.

      
If VIPOK™ uses stock inventory lenses, can it really be precise?
Each stock lens is individually calculated for how corneas change from center to periphery, and for the differences between flat and steep corneas. Selecting from a fixed chart also avoids errors from transferring and recalculating fitting data. Fit is confirmed chairside with fluorescein evaluation.

      
How do patients know they received genuine VIPOK™ lenses?
Every lens dispensed by a certified VIPOK doctor is individually shrink-wrapped. Patients can ask to see the original package before taking lenses home.

      
Where is the safety and prescribing information?
See Important Safety Information. The package insert for each lens is the authoritative source for indications, contraindications, warnings and adverse reactions. [Package inserts to be supplied by VIPOK]

Important Safety Information

    
Draft. Written from standard orthokeratology labeling. Before launch, VIPOK must replace it with the approved package-insert text for each lens. [Package inserts to be supplied by VIPOK]

    

      
Rx only. Caution: US federal law restricts this device to sale by or on the order of a licensed practitioner. VIPOK lenses must be fitted and followed by a licensed eye care practitioner.

      
Indications

      
See the package insert for each lens’s approved indications, including the range of refractive error, astigmatism, wearing schedule and patient age. [From the package insert]

      
Results are temporary and vary

      
Orthokeratology temporarily reshapes the cornea. Vision gradually returns toward its original level if the lenses are not worn as prescribed. Results vary from patient to patient, and not every patient reaches the same level of vision.

      
Do not wear ortho-k lenses if you have

      
Contraindications typically include the following; confirm against the package insert.

      
    
        
  • An infection or inflammation of the eye, or any eye disease, injury or abnormality that affects the cornea, conjunctiva or eyelids
    • 
        
  • Severe dry eye, or reduced corneal sensitivity
    • 
        
  • A systemic disease that may affect the eye or be made worse by contact lens wear
    • 
        
  • An allergy to any ingredient in the lens care solutions
    • 
        
  • Any condition that prevents you from following the wearing, care and follow-up instructions
    • 
      

      
Warnings

      
    
        
  • Wearing contact lenses, especially overnight, can cause serious eye infection (microbial keratitis) that may lead to permanent vision loss.
    • 
        
  • Remove your lenses and contact your eye care practitioner right away if you have eye pain, redness, discharge, light sensitivity, blurred vision or any other problem.
    • 
        
  • Never rinse or store lenses in tap water, and never use saliva. Clean, disinfect and store lenses only as your practitioner directs.
    • 
        
  • Keep every follow-up visit, even when your vision seems good.
    • 
      

      
Possible side effects

      
These may include eye discomfort, redness, tearing, dryness, glare or halos around lights, fluctuating vision, corneal staining or abrasion, and eye infection.

      
Report a problem

      
Tell your eye care practitioner and VIPOK at customer_service@vipok.com. You can also report serious problems to the FDA MedWatch program at fda.gov/medwatch or 1-800-FDA-1088.

Some legacy images, such as corneal topography maps and fluorescein photos, are low resolution and carry information that is only partly described in text. We are replacing them with higher-resolution versions and fuller descriptions.

      
Feedback
